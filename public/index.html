Questions about a dark spot

Use Pair and the existing screen probe. The sky shows normalized brightness, not a photograph or a transmitted-energy meter.

Can two lit openings make a dark spot?

Choose Pair with point openings, equal strengths and the same source phase. Set the probe to u = 0.5 and v = 0. Read the arriving arrows as well as the brightness.

Read an answer

Yes. The centers at x = −0.5 and +0.5 give opposite arriving phases at that spot. Their signed components cancel. Each contribution exists even though the sum is dark.

Does a wider gap push that cancellation outward?

Keep the Pair mask. Widen its gap and move the horizontal probe until the first dark spot returns. Watch the coordinates rather than guessing from the sky’s glow.

Read an answer

It moves inward. For two equal point sources separated by gap d, the first horizontal null is at u = 1/(2d). A gap of 1 gives 0.5; a gap of 1.5 gives about 0.333. The rectangular opening envelope is a separate factor.

Does closing one opening restore full brightness?

Choose Ring, point openings and only the chosen opening. Inspect the center. Compare it with lighting all six openings.

Read an answer

No. This toy keeps the complete six-opening normalization. One full-amplitude source contributes brightness 1/36, about 2.778%, at the center. It does not renormalize the single surviving source to 100%. Closing sources and changing the common scale are different operations.

Does a moved triangle move its dark spots?

Choose Triangle at the center, then either translated triangle. Keep the same point-opening settings, illumination and sky probe.

Read an answer

A common translation adds the same phase factor to every arriving contribution. That rotates their sum without changing its magnitude, so ideal array brightness stays the same at the same sky coordinates. Moving the mask is not moving the sky probe.

Is the outer span enough to predict the sky?

Compare the evenly spaced line, close inner pair and two close outer pairs at the same sky spot.

Read an answer

All three span from -0.75 to +0.75 mask units, but their internal gaps differ. Every relative phase matters to the sum. The same width and source count do not guarantee the same interference pattern.

Does a diagonal pair have a wider gap?

Compare horizontal and diagonal pairs. The diagonal displacement is 0.24 across and 0.32 up.

Read an answer

Its length is 0.4 mask units, because 0.24 squared plus 0.32 squared is 0.16. That matches the horizontal gap. Direction changes while length stays fixed; the three pair centers stay fixed too.
